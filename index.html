Seven straits · 34.5 mb/d

Some straits have a detour.
Three of them have nothing.

A map tells you where the sea is narrow. It does not tell you which narrow places have another way round. Those are two completely different kinds of fragile, and they look identical from above.

Close one and find out. Every barrel that can reroute will — by sea if there is a sea route, then through a bypass pipeline until that pipeline is full. What fits in neither does not arrive late. It does not move at all.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this page traces to one of three published sources. The model is a calibrated snapshot, not a live routing engine — transit times are estimates tuned to published anchors, accurate to about a day.

  • US Energy Information Administration, World Oil Transit Chokepoints — chokepoint volumes, bypass-pipeline capacities, and the country-level flows through Hormuz. First half of 2025 unless noted.
  • IMF PortWatch — chokepoint coordinates, from the satellite-AIS chokepoints database. Downloaded as a static snapshot; this page makes no network requests.
  • Verschuur et al., Systemic impacts of disruptions at maritime chokepoints, Nature Communications (2025) — the three reroutability classes, which are the argument this page is making.

What this model leaves out

  • Oil only, in one unit. Everything here is million barrels per day of crude and refined products. LNG is measured in billion cubic feet per day, so it is excluded — two units on one screen would make the stranded total unaddable.
  • Malacca is understated. Only Persian Gulf origins are modelled, so the traffic shown through the Strait of Malacca is below the 23.2 mb/d EIA reports. West African and Atlantic-basin crude also uses it.
  • Two flows are illustrative. The Houston–Rotterdam and Kozmino–Qingdao volumes are order-of-magnitude only. They exist so there is something on the map that never moves; their exact size is not load-bearing. Two more are marked derived in the source data — apportioned or residual, so the named flows reconcile to EIA's published Hormuz total.
  • Scarce capacity is shared evenly. When several flows want the same bypass, each gets a share proportional to what it is trying to move. Real allocation would be settled by contract and price; this page does not speculate about whose oil moves first.
